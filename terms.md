## Part C. What a forecast in the app means

**C1. Purpose.** The app takes a route (a start, a summit and optional waypoints or a GPX track), a departure time and a pace, estimates when you reach each elevation step, and fetches a weather forecast for each step at its elevation. From that it derives the freezing level, the snow line, the cloud base, wind chill, a storm-risk window, daylight and a plain verdict, and shows them on a 3D terrain model. Pro adds longer forecasts, unlimited plans, GPX import, alerts, offline copies and a PDF report.

**C2. The weather data.** Forecasts come from a public numerical weather model with a grid of 2.5 to 9 km depending on the region. Such a model cannot see a single valley, ridge or slope. Hourly detail covers about two days; later hours are interpolated between six-hourly values. Outside the Nordic countries the service provides no gust speed and no precipitation or thunder probability; the app estimates gusts and marks them, and infers storm risk from the model's weather symbols.

**C3. The terrain data.** Heights come from a public bare-earth elevation model with samples about 30 m apart and a vertical error that can reach 16 m, more on steep ground and at sharp summits. It holds **no snow cover, no trees, no buildings and no glaciers' current state**.

**C4. Derived values.** Freezing level, snow line, cloud base, wind chill, sun on the slope, turnaround time, the week's best window and the verdict are computed from the model and the terrain with standard meteorological approximations. They are estimates with the same limits as their inputs, and they can be wrong.

**C5. Alerts.** Forecast change alerts run only when iOS chooses to wake the app in the background. They may be late or not arrive at all. Never rely on an alert as your warning of changing weather.

**C6. Things the app never does.** The app does not measure the weather, does not receive official warnings, does not know about avalanche danger, snow conditions, rockfall, river levels or trail closures, and does not track you during the climb.

**C7. You decide.** Every screen is an estimate and only a planning aid. **You carry full responsibility for every decision on the mountain.** Read the official mountain forecast and warnings for your area, carry the right equipment, and turn back when conditions demand it. Do not use the app for aviation, for rescue or emergency work, as evidence in legal matters, or for any task where a wrong forecast could cause injury, damage or loss.
